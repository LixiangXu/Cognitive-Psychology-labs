Star Cluster Experiment

You are an astronomer classifying newly discovered, highly variable star clusters. You must learn to distinguish Star Cluster Alpha from Star Cluster Beta.

Each pattern of dots you see is one star cluster. You will classify each one with the F and J keys.

The experiment takes about 10 minutes. Clicking Start means you agree to take part.

A random ID has been filled in.

Part 1: Learning

Each trial starts with a + in the center. Then a star cluster (a pattern of dots) appears and stays on the screen until you answer.

Decide which star cluster it is:

Press F for Star Cluster Alpha
Press J for Star Cluster Beta

At first you will have to guess. After each answer you will see Correct! or Incorrect! — use this feedback to learn the two categories.

Keep your left index finger on F and your right index finger on J.

Press SPACE to begin.

Alpha (F)Beta (J)

Part 2: Test

Well done! From now on there will be no feedback.

You will see more dot patterns. Some may look familiar and some will be new. Classify each one as best you can:

Press F for Star Cluster Alpha
Press J for Star Cluster Beta

If you are not sure, go with your first impression.

Press SPACE to begin the test.

Thank you!

The prototypes were never shown during training. Posner & Keele (1968) found that people classify the unseen prototype about as well as the patterns they trained on, and better than new distortions — as if they had built up an abstract average of each category. Random patterns belong to neither category, so no answer is correct; the table shows how often you called them Alpha.
